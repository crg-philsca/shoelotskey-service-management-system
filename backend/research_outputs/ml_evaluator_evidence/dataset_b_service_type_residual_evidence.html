1. Analysis Purpose & Mandatory Research Constraints

This audit was conducted to satisfy the specific evaluator requirement: “Residual or error analysis by service type.” In accordance with strict academic integrity standards:

  • No data regeneration or manipulation: Analysis is executed strictly against the sealed 1,500-record Dataset B final test set (dataset_b_test_1500.csv).
  • No model retraining or retuning: The finalized frozen Random Forest model (dataset_b_selected_model.pkl, 300 estimators, max depth 12) was audited as-is without parameter adjustment.
  • Zero test record exclusion: All 1,500 test records are preserved; no difficult or outlier records were pruned.
  • Non-mutually-exclusive grouping: In authentic workshop operations, an order may contain multiple distinct services (e.g., Basic Cleaning + Full Reglue). Orders are assigned to a service category whenever quantity > 0 for that service. Consequently, multi-service orders appear in multiple categories, and the sum of subgroup sample sizes ($\sum N = 2,664$) exceeds the total unique test size ($1,500$).
Verification Protocol: Residuals are defined strictly as Residual = Actual Duration − Predicted Duration. A positive residual indicates model underprediction (actual workshop turnaround exceeded prediction), while a negative residual indicates overprediction (model predicted longer than actual turnaround).

7. Manuscript-Ready Interpretation (Chapter 4 Text)

The following text is provided for direct insertion below the table in Chapter 4 of the manuscript:

The subgroup residual and error analysis by service type on the Dataset B simulated operational final test set ($N = 1,500$) demonstrates consistent predictive stability across all six service categories. Because operational service orders frequently involve bundled maintenance tasks, categories were evaluated on a non-mutually-exclusive basis wherein an order was included in a service subgroup whenever its respective service quantity exceeded zero (yielding a cumulative category count of $\sum N = 2,664$). Across all categories, Mean Absolute Error (MAE) remained exceptionally low, ranging from a minimum of 0.0006 days for orders involving Color Renewal ($N = 35$) to a maximum of 0.1193 days for orders containing Minor Restoration ($N = 274$). The primary service category, Basic Cleaning ($N = 1,483$), achieved an MAE of 0.0806 days ($pprox 1.93$ hours) and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 0.4058 days, closely mirroring the overall dataset benchmark of 0.0815 days.

Variance across service types remained tightly bounded, with RMSE peaking at 0.5419 days for Unyellowing ($N = 185$) and 0.5363 days for Minor Restoration ($N = 274$). This minor elevation in dispersion reflects the broader scheduling variability associated with chemical curing and multi-stage restoration relative to routine cleaning. Directional analysis of mean residuals (Actual − Predicted) indicated minimal systematic bias across all service types. Full Reglue ($N = 569$) and Minor Reglue ($N = 118$) exhibited slight negative residuals (−0.0205 days and −0.0173 days, respectively), reflecting a negligible tendency toward overprediction where predicted turnaround was marginally longer than simulated workshop completion. Conversely, Basic Cleaning ($+0.0025$ days), Minor Restoration ($+0.0033$ days), and Unyellowing ($+0.0100$ days) displayed slight positive residuals, representing minute underprediction.

Tolerance accuracy across operational thresholds confirmed high practical reliability across all service types. Within the ±1-day operational window, accuracy ranged from 95.61% for Full Reglue orders to 100.00% for Color Renewal orders, with Basic Cleaning achieving 97.17%. Expanding the tolerance to ±2 days elevated accuracy above 97.45% for all service types, while ±3-day accuracy exceeded 98.92% across all categories. Crucially, 100.00% of orders across every service type were predicted within a ±5-day margin. These results substantiate that the model's predictive precision is robust across distinct craftwork types within the simulated operational environment, without exhibiting severe service-specific failure modes or anomalous prediction skew.
