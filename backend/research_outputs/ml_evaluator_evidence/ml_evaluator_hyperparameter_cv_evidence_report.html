12. Methodological Synthesis: Dataset A vs. Dataset B Validation Designs

Authoritative Defense Talking Point

When presenting this work to an academic defense panel, students must clearly distinguish why two different validation strategies were used:

  • Why Dataset A used 5-Fold Expanding-Window Cross-Validation: In small empirical time-series datasets ($N=119$, with 93 development orders), random K-Fold splits would cause temporal leakage (future orders predicting past orders). A single train/test split would be vulnerable to sample size variance. The expanding window verified that the model remained stable as the intake window expanded from August to December 2025.
  • Why Dataset B used a 70/15/15 Holdout Split: In large-scale operational validation ($N=10,000$), 1,500 validation orders provide massive statistical power for evaluating 324 hyperparameter combinations without needing repeated k-fold folds. The separate 1,500 sealed test set guaranteed that zero tuning leakage occurred into the final reported operational test score ($MAE = 0.0815$ days).

17. Manuscript-Ready Academic Narrative

Ready for Chapter 3 / Chapter 4 Integration

The machine learning development in this study was structured across two complementary stages: empirical historical benchmark modeling (Dataset A) and production operational scaling (Dataset B). To evaluate empirical model stability while strictly preventing look-ahead temporal leakage, the 93 development records of Historical Dataset A were evaluated using a 5-fold expanding-window cross-validation strategy. Rather than randomly shuffling observations, records were sorted in chronological order of intake (August 15 – December 29, 2025). Fold 1 was initialized with 18 intake orders and validated on the subsequent 15 consecutive orders. Across successive folds, the training partition expanded monotonically to 33, 48, 64, and 78 records, while validation sets moved forward in time without overlapping previous training periods. The empirical model achieved a cross-validation Mean Absolute Error of 8.26 ± 2.11 days (persisted historical benchmark: 8.1541 days), an RMSE of 10.45 ± 2.96 days (persisted: 10.3225 days), and a mean R² of 0.26 ± 0.31 (persisted: 0.2679), with the 26 January 2026 holdout records remaining entirely sequestered as a locked test set. In parallel, systematic hyperparameter optimization was conducted for the 10,000-record operational Dataset B across 324 candidate combinations using a 70/15/15 three-way holdout split. The selected configuration of 300 estimators, maximum depth of 12, minimum split of 10, minimum leaf of 4, and full feature consideration (max_features=1.0) achieved an operational validation MAE of 0.0641 days and a locked test MAE of 0.0815 days (≈ 1.96 hours), with 97.20% of orders predicted within a ±1-day window and 100% within ±5 days, successfully establishing the dual-stage predictive pipeline for the Shoelotskey Service Management System.
