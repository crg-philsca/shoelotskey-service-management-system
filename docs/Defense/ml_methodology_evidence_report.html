Executive Narrative: Why the Methodology Evolved

1. The Problem with Dataset A (N = 119 Empirical Orders)

The development team initially gathered 119 human-verified historical paper claim slips from Shoelotskey. The available recorded target on these slips was Claim Date minus Date Received.

When machine learning models were trained on this data, the best candidate (Random Forest) achieved an MAE of 8.35 days and a 5-day tolerance accuracy of only 42.3%.

Root Cause: Shoes were completed and sitting ready on the shop shelf within 1 to 25 days, but customers frequently did not claim them until weeks or months later (some claiming after 30, 45, or 60+ days). The models were learning human customer pickup habits rather than actual repair duration.

2. The Chairperson's Directive & Solution (Dataset B)

During capstone research review, the thesis committee chairperson and faculty panel explicitly pointed out:

“Predicting customer pickup behavior is outside shop operational control. The service management system must predict Ready-for-Release Date (when the shop finishes the shoes), not customer claiming latency. Furthermore, 119 records lack the statistical support for complex multi-pair and rush permutations.”

The chairperson instructed the team to generate Dataset B (10,000 simulated operational records) using verified shop business rules, non-additive service tiers, pair-level max rules, rush fee acceleration, and calendar rollovers.

Methodological Preservation: Dataset A was NOT deleted. It is preserved in the thesis as the empirical historical baseline of customer pickup latency, while Dataset B is the authorized operational dataset.

Model Selection Decision Rationale (Preserved from Phase 3A):
1. Full Transparency Regarding MAE:

The Decision Tree achieved an MAE of 0.0616 days, which was marginally lower than Random Forest's 0.0641 days (a difference of only ≈ 3.6 minutes). The research documentation explicitly acknowledges this and does not falsely claim Random Forest had the lowest MAE.

2. Why Random Forest was Selected (Decisive Criteria):
  • Lowest RMSE (0.2994 days): Outperformed Decision Tree (0.3093 days), penalizing large tail errors from multi-day holiday rollovers.
  • Highest Variance Explained (R² = 0.9987): Highest explanatory power on operational duration.
  • Ensemble Generalization: A single tree carries high variance. Random Forest averages 300 decorrelated trees, mitigating overfitting risk across edge cases.
  • Predeclared Tie-Breaker: Specification predeclared RMSE as decisive when MAE values were within ±0.005 days.
