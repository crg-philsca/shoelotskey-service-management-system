Methodological Principle: Empirical Verification Before Synthetic Augmentation

The 119 validated historical records represent genuine, authenticated Shoelotskey business history. In academic research, empirical data should never be discarded merely because an initial machine learning model exhibits modest performance. Before considering synthetic data augmentation, a researcher must first evaluate the empirical model against rigorous benchmarks, identify the root cause of the error, and formally document why real-world performance deviates from theoretical expectations.

Defensible Sequence (What We Are Executing)

Freeze the 26-record test set → Evaluate Random Forest against 5 distinct baselines → Diagnose empirical target constraints (Customer Pickup Latency) → Determine if synthetic simulation is academically justified → Obtain chairperson approval → Retrain & evaluate on separate partitions → Update UI & manuscript.

Flawed Sequence (What Must Be Avoided)

Generating 5,000 synthetic rows immediately to "manufacture a passing score" without baseline diagnosis. A model trained on synthetic data and tested only on synthetic data proves only that it learned the generator's mathematical assumptions—not that it accurately predicts real Shoelotskey operations.

2. Academic Defense Diagnosis: The "Order-to-Claim" Target Limitation

A. Physical Reality of Shoelotskey Paper Receipts

In Shoelotskey's physical manual workflow prior to this system, paper claim slips recorded exactly two handwritten dates:

  • Date Received: The exact date the customer brought footwear into the shop.
  • Claimed Date: The date the customer returned to settle payment and retrieve the footwear.

The physical slips never recorded when the artisan completed the repair. Therefore, calculating (claimed_date - date_received) inevitably captures Customer Pickup Latency (e.g., customers waiting weeks after completion before claiming their items).

B. Why This Defends the Research Score

When panel members ask: "Why is R² low and why did the model not hit 85% within ±2 days?", the defensible answer is:

Authoritative Student Defense Talking Point:

“Our 119 records are 100% genuine Shoelotskey empirical data. We deliberately rejected fabricating synthetic timestamps to artificially inflate R². The low R² proves a critical domain finding: customer pickup turnaround cannot be modeled solely by shoe repair features because customer schedule flexibility is an unobserved human behavioral factor. That is precisely why our new software introduces real-time stage tracking (Mark as Ready for Release) to capture pure service turnaround for future models.”

3. Dataset Governance Framework: Separating Empirical from Synthetic

If the capstone chairperson and panel approve synthetic data generation to study service turnaround under controlled conditions, the research design must maintain strict architectural separation between historical empirical data and simulated data:

Dataset A — Empirical Historical

Real World
  • Sample Size: 119 validated physical job orders.
  • Partitions: 93 Development (2025) / 26 Frozen Holdout (2026).
  • Prediction Target: Historical Order-to-Claim turnaround.
  • Scientific Role: Primary empirical dataset; demonstrates manual record-keeping limitations and establishes the need for digital tracking.
  • Integrity Rule: Never modified, re-split, or inflated with fake rows.

Dataset B — Operational Simulation

Simulated
  • Sample Size: Defined by formal methodology (e.g., 2,500 – 5,000 scenarios).
  • Partitions: Independent train / validation / test splits.
  • Prediction Target: Pure service turnaround (Date Received → Ready for Release).
  • Scientific Role: Evaluates Random Forest performance under controlled operational rules and queue mechanics.
  • Integrity Rule: Clearly identified as simulated; never described as actual Shoelotskey transactions.
