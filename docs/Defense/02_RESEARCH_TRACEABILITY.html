#1 Manual vs. Digital Comparative Analysis
ALIGNED
Problem Manual logbooks cause record fragmentation, slow claiming retrieval (3–10 min), and frequent encoding errors (2–3 daily corrections).
Objective 1 Conduct a comparative analysis between existing manual paper-based system and developed digital system in terms of accessibility, retrieval time, and accuracy.
Methodology 10 retrieval trials using identical order records in manual logbook vs. digital search; daily observation of duplicate/incomplete records over 2 weeks.
Chapter 4 Table 2: Mean retrieval time reduced from 5.20 min to 0.41 min (92.12% reduction). Table 3: Duplicate entries reduced from 2–3/day to 0.
Chapter 5 Conclusion 1: Digital centralization eliminated retrieval bottlenecks and manual duplication errors.
Slide Deck Slide 9: Highlights 92.12% retrieval reduction and automated field validation with zero duplicates.
#2 Machine Learning Service Turnaround Prediction
PARTIAL / REQUIRES CLARIFICATION
Problem Service completion estimation relies entirely on subjective staff memory, causing inconsistent customer pickup dates and workload imbalances.
Objective 2 Evaluate the machine learning model for predicting service turnaround duration and release dates using historical service data based on regression metrics.
Methodology 119 validated orders from 714 paper receipts; chronological split: 93 dev (Aug–Dec 2025) for 5-fold expanding-window CV, 26 locked test (Jan 2026); 13 features; target: claimed_date - date_received.
Chapter 4 Table 5: 10 representative test rows. Table 6: Complete test set: MAE = 8.50 days, RMSE = 10.11 days, R² = 0.129. Benchmark ≥ 0.85 not achieved; designated strictly advisory.
Chapter 5 Conclusion 2: Deployed as advisory estimate under Option B; official release dates remain determined by deterministic Business Rules.
