1. Critical Terminology Audit: Completion Date vs. Historical Target

⚡
Root Semantic Confusion Identified in Chapter 1: Throughout Chapter 1 (Problem Statement, Purpose, Objectives), the manuscript repeatedly uses phrases such as "service completion date estimation", "completion duration", and "predicting completion time".

Empirical Reality from Level 1 Code & Level 2 Data: Historical paper logbooks at Shoelotskey did NOT record technician completion timestamps (the moment cleaning or reglue was finished). They recorded only Date Received (order intake) and Claimed Date (when the customer picked up their shoes). Therefore:
  • The historical ML target is strictly: historical_order_to_claim_turnaround_days = (claimed_date - date_received).days.
  • This target reflects customer pickup/turnaround behavior, not pure technician bench time.
  • The developed digital system separately captures released_at timestamps, providing the empirical foundation for true completion modeling in future research.
  • The production ML prediction must be framed as an advisory turnaround estimate under the Option B dual-date architecture, while official promised dates remain deterministic business rules.

2. Conceptual Framework Audit: Removal of Continuous Learning Fallacy

⚠
Unsupported Claim in Original Conceptual Framework (Figure 1 narrative, p. 9): "The feedback loop allows the system to learn from its mistakes in real-time. If an ML prediction was wrong, the actual completion time is fed back in real-time to automatically improve future accuracy."

Why This Must Be Removed:
  • The production Scikit-Learn model is statically serialized in backend/completion_model.pkl. Automatic online backpropagation / continuous streaming learning does not exist in standard tabular Random Forest regressors.
  • In production micro-enterprise systems, uncontrolled automatic retraining introduces catastrophic data leakage and model drift from anomalous inputs.
  • Authoritative Replacement Concept: Validated operational records are archived upon customer pickup. The business owner/administrator can periodically trigger a controlled batch retraining routine via /api/historical/train, with new models evaluated and validated before deployment.
