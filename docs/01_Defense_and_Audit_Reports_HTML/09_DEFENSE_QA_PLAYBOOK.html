🤖 Battleground A: Machine Learning, Target Definition & The 10-Record Sample Trap
Q-01 Target Semantics & Empirical Reality
"Does your Machine Learning model predict the exact date the technician finishes repairing the shoes?"
❌ WHAT NOT TO SAY: "Yes, our Random Forest model predicts when the shoe technician completes the repair job inside the workshop."
✅ GOLDEN CANDIDATE DEFENSE: "No, esteemed panel. In our historical receipt review spanning August 2025 through January 2026, the shop's physical paper claim tickets recorded only two reliable transaction timestamps: date_received and claimed_date. The shop maintained no internal time-logging when a repairman finished sewing or gluing. Therefore, to ensure 100% empirical truthfulness without synthetic data hallucination, our model targets historical customer turnaround duration in days: turnaround_days = claimed_date - date_received. In our user interface under Option B, this is presented as an advisory prediction alongside an editable estimated ready date."
Source: Chapter 3 (pp. 32–33) & backend/historical_ml_engine.py

Q-03 Negative R-Squared Defense
"Your R-squared on the test set is negative (-0.0208). Doesn't a negative R² mean your model is completely useless?"
❌ WHAT NOT TO SAY: "No, negative R-squared is a software glitch in scikit-learn / Our model is actually 85% accurate."
✅ GOLDEN CANDIDATE DEFENSE: "In academic machine learning, when evaluating an out-of-sample holdout without re-centering to the test set mean, R² can mathematically fall below zero if high-variance outliers skew the total sum of squares. In our January 2026 test set, three unannounced customer pick-up delays (where customers left completed shoes for over 45 days) distorted the squared residual sum. However, our Mean Absolute Error (MAE = 8.56 days) demonstrates that the Random Forest model outperformed simple mean duration heuristics by 0.78 days across all standard transactions. Furthermore, because we architected the system under Option B, the prediction is never forced upon operations; it provides an advisory baseline that staff can freely calibrate."
Source: Chapter 4 (p. 111) & backend/research_outputs/ml_final/05_final_metrics.json
Q-04 Continuous Learning Fallacy
"Does your system learn continuously in real-time as soon as a staff member enters a new job order?"
❌ WHAT NOT TO SAY: "Yes, our neural network updates its weights in real-time on every click."
✅ GOLDEN CANDIDATE DEFENSE: "No, Sir/Ma'am. In production software engineering, real-time online backpropagation on micro-datasets causes catastrophic forgetting, data poisoning, and severe model drift. In Shoelotskey, the production model is statically serialized in completion_model.pkl. When orders are completed, their verified attributes are archived into relational storage. The business owner can trigger a controlled, validated administrative batch retraining routine via /api/historical/train, ensuring thorough cross-validation before any model artifact replaces production."
Source: Conceptual Framework (Figure 1, p. 9) & backend/main.py line 812

Q-06 Offline Fallback vs Production Integrity
"What happens if the internet goes down at Shoelotskey? Does the production system fail over to SQLite?"
❌ WHAT NOT TO SAY: "Yes, the cloud server automatically switches to local SQLite without anyone noticing."
✅ GOLDEN CANDIDATE DEFENSE: "In production on AWS RDS / Heroku, the system enforces a strict fail-closed security policy: if the PostgreSQL cluster is unreachable, the API returns HTTP 503 Service Unavailable rather than silently degrading. Silent failover to a local SQLite database would cause split-brain data corruption and desynchronize financial records across multiple shop counters. SQLite fallback is strictly restricted to local development and offline defense presentation continuity, guarded by our P0-5 environment lock."
Source: backend/database.py lines 28–45 & SYSTEM_GROUND_TRUTH.md
