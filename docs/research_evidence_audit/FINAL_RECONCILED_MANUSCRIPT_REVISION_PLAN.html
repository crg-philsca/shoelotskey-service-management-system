Step 2 — Critical Research & Machine Learning Directives Resolved

Binding methodological decisions governing Chapter 3 research procedures, Chapter 4 results, and capstone oral defense.

1. Exclusive Focus on Dataset B Operational Model
The formal machine learning performance evaluation in Chapter 4 must focus strictly and exclusively on Dataset B (10,000 simulated operational records). Dataset A ($N=119$ historical paper receipts) must NOT be reintroduced as a second formal ML evaluation. Instead, Dataset A is documented strictly in Chapter 3 as historical diagnostic provenance, demonstrating why historical paper claim dates cannot measure craft completion times and establishing the 35 canonical workshop service templates.
2. Dual-Date Operational Architecture Distinction
The manuscript must clearly differentiate the two release dates computed by the system:
  • Official Expected Release Date (Standard Release Date): Deterministic rule-based calculation enforcing shop commitments (base turnaround days + rush modifier + workshop calendar rollovers). This date governs customer claims and payment policies.
  • ML Predicted Release Date: Continuous predictive estimate generated in real-time by the Random Forest Regressor at /api/predict. It serves an internal advisory role for workload balancing.
3. Mandatory Generalization Caveat (Simulated vs. Real-World Accuracy)
Researchers must never claim that high test performance ($R^2 = 0.9976$, $ ext{MAE} = 0.0815 ext{ days}$) proves real-world prediction accuracy. The manuscript must explicitly disclose that this high accuracy represents the Random Forest's mathematical convergence on deterministic workshop business rules embedded in Dataset B's generation logic. Live operational accuracy against human customer pickup delays remains to be evaluated once historical `For Release` timestamps accumulate over multi-month live operations.
