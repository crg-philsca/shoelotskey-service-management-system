Check 2: Record Retrieval Benchmark Authenticity

The manuscript (Chapter 4, Section 4.1, Tables 17 and 19, pages 146–150) documents ten (10) paired stopwatch trials comparing the physical paper logbook against the centralized digital system. The authentic findings are:

  • Manual Retrieval Time: Mean = 5.20 minutes, SD = 2.10 minutes, Range = 3.00 to 10.00 minutes.
  • Digital System Retrieval Time: Mean = 0.41 minutes (24.6 seconds), SD = 0.09 minutes, Range = 0.30 to 0.50 minutes.
  • Mean Improvement: 4.79 minutes reduction, representing a 92.12% efficiency gain ($p < 0.001$).
The alternative numbers cited in previous text (127.8s vs 2.74s) originated from a misunderstanding of automated database query latency logged in sqlite_legacy_snapshot.json (where a bulk SELECT query executed in 127.83 milliseconds). That technical log was erroneously misquoted as manual stopwatch seconds.

Check 5: Simulation Assumptions & Split Configuration

The operational dataset generation script (generate_dataset_b.py) operates deterministically using seed 20261002 over 10,000 synthetic records. It samples service combinations from the empirical distributions of Dataset A (119 receipts) and applies workshop business rules:

  • Basic Cleaning: 10 days standard (1 day if rush).
  • Reglue & Unyellowing: 20 days standard.
  • Restoration & Color Renewal: 25 days standard.
  • Calendar Rollover: Orders landing on Sundays or shop holidays (Holy Week, Christmas/New Year) roll forward to the next business day.
  • Data Partition: 7,000 train (70%), 1,500 validation (15%), 1,500 sealed test (15%).
  • Noise: Zero Gaussian noise. The model's test performance ($ ext{MAE} = 0.0815 ext{ days}$, $R^2 = 0.9976$) reflects its ability to learn these deterministic rules.
