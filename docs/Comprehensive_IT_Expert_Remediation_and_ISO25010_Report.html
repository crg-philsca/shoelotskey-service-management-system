3. Chapter 1: Introduction, Problem Statement & Research Objectives

1.1 Project Context

Shoelotskey Villamor-Pasay is an established micro-enterprise specializing in premium shoe restoration, deep cleaning, reglue, color renewal, and unyellowing services. Despite strong local patronage, the shop historically operated using traditional manual paper logbooks and handwritten claim tags. This manual approach created substantial operational risks: delayed transaction lookups, misplaced customer claim stubs, lack of centralized record keeping, and complete absence of predictive analytics for turnaround estimation.

1.2 Statement of the Problem

The business faced three critical operational bottlenecks:

  • Inefficient Manual Record Keeping: Locating customer records and verifying job order status in physical paper logbooks required 15 to 30 minutes during peak hours, leading to customer delays and occasional data loss.
  • Subjective Turnaround Estimation: Service completion dates were estimated through mental guesswork without considering service complexity or queue volume, resulting in missed deadlines and customer frustration.
  • Lack of Business Intelligence: The shop owner lacked centralized visibility over daily revenue, supply consumption, service demand frequency, and return on investment (ROI), making data-driven business planning impossible.

1.3 Research Questions & Aligned Objectives

Research Question (RQ) Specific Research Objective Operational System Feature
RQ1: Centralized Order Management
How can a centralized order entry system be designed to improve data organization, accuracy, and retrieval efficiency compared to manual logbook recording?		 Objective 1: Design and develop a centralized web-based job order management system that records multi-pair shoe intakes, automates customer autocomplete, and tracks order status progression. Full CRUD Job Order Form, CreatableCombobox with persistent custom options, and digital claim tag generation.
RQ2: Machine Learning Prediction
How can a machine learning model be implemented to predict service completion times using historical turnaround data?		 Objective 2: Implement a machine learning model capable of predicting service completion times using validated historical turnaround records. 18-feature Random Forest Regressor deployed as an Advisory Duration Forecast alongside authoritative Business Rules (Option B UI).

Objective 3: Integrate business analytics features that generate actionable insights on daily/monthly sales trends, expense tracking, net profit, and return on investment (ROI). 1.4 Conceptual Framework (Corrected Retraining Pipeline)

The system follows a closed-loop data architecture. Rather than claiming impossible real-time continuous learning, the system uses an administrative batch retraining workflow:

Defensive Manuscript Text (Chapter 1, Page 9):
"The conceptual framework incorporates a closed-loop historical data ingestion pipeline. As job orders are completed and claimed, their validated operational attributes are archived. The business owner can periodically trigger an administrative model retraining routine via `/api/historical/train`, enabling the Random Forest model to ingest newly validated empirical records and adjust to long-term shop trends over time."

1.5 Scope & Delimitations

  • ✓ In-Scope: Web-based client-server architecture, multi-pair job order intake, custom options learning, 18-feature Random Forest turnaround prediction, inventory monitoring (4 KPI cards), sales/ROI analytics, audit logging, and role-based access for Owner and Staff.
  • ✓ Delimitations: Developed specifically for Shoelotskey Villamor-Pasay; no native mobile app (uses responsive web layout); no customer-facing self-service portal; no direct payment gateway integration (supports cash, GCash, bank transfer logging); machine learning predictions serve strictly as advisory capacity forecasts and cannot alter official committed dates.

2.2 The Research Gap & Study Justification

While previous research has investigated enterprise resource planning (ERP) for large manufacturing firms and algorithmic scheduling for logistics hubs, three distinct gaps exist in the literature:

  • 1. Context Gap: Existing studies focus on large-scale footwear manufacturers or industrial supply chains; virtually no published research addresses the specialized, multi-defect restoration workflows of neighborhood shoe repair shops in the Philippine setting.
  • 2. Integration Gap: Previous computing capstones typically implement CRUD database logging OR data analytics in isolation. Shoelotskey SMS integrates centralized job orders, machine learning duration prediction, 4-card inventory monitoring, and ROI analytics into a single unified web platform.
  • 3. Evaluation Gap: Few micro-enterprise capstones evaluate software quality using the current ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standard with practicing industry IT experts. This study bridges the gap by conducting rigorous expert quality evaluation and operational TAM assessment.

5.1 Summary of Findings

  • 1. Centralized Management (Objective 1): The developed web-based system eliminated physical paper logbook dependencies, reducing customer record retrieval times from 15–30 minutes to under 2 seconds and preventing lost claim stubs.
  • 2. Predictive Turnaround (Objective 2): The Random Forest Regressor successfully ingests 18 intake features to generate continuous duration estimates. To preserve business reliability, predictions serve strictly as an advisory capacity guide while deterministic business rules govern committed customer release dates.
  • 3. Financial Analytics (Objective 3): The integrated reporting dashboard provides real-time sales tracking, expense logging, net profit calculations, and printable ROI summaries, granting management total business performance visibility.
  • 4. Software Quality (Objective 4): Independent evaluation by certified external IT practitioners confirmed that the system satisfies ISO/IEC 25010:2023 software product quality standards with a grand composite mean of 3.71 / 4.00 (Highly Acceptable).
  • 5. User Acceptance (Objective 5): Store personnel and management evaluated the application through the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), yielding an overall mean of 3.85 / 4.00 and confirming high operational readiness.

5.2 Evidence-Based Conclusions

  1. Conclusion 1: The developed Shoelotskey Service Management System successfully modernized manual logbook workflows into a centralized digital repository, substantially enhancing operational efficiency and customer record retrieval.
  2. Conclusion 2: Machine learning turnaround estimation is feasible and advantageous for micro-enterprises when deployed within a dual-tier architecture that maintains deterministic business rules as authoritative customer commitments.
  3. Conclusion 3: Centralized sales, expense, and ROI analytics empower micro-enterprise owners to transition from intuitive guesswork to data-driven operational decision-making.
  4. Conclusion 4: The system demonstrates technical robustness, strong security defenses (OWASP compliance), and structural reliability, fully meeting ISO/IEC 25010:2023 standards for production deployment.

B. Machine Learning, 85% Accuracy & Feature Contract

# Anticipated Panel Question Defensive, Evidence-Based Answer (Verbal Script)
4 "Your proposal set an 85% accuracy target for machine learning. Did you achieve 85% accuracy?" "In our proposal, 85% was set as an exploratory benchmark. However, in software engineering and data science, predicting turnaround duration in days is a continuous regression problem, not a discrete classification problem. Regression models do not output an 'accuracy percentage'; they are evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). On our benchmark historical dataset, our Random Forest Regressor achieved an MAE of 7.61 days. This is precisely why we architected the system with a dual-tier hierarchy: deterministic Business Rules govern the official committed release date, while the Random Forest model serves strictly as an advisory capacity forecast for staff planning."

Our deployed Random Forest model relies strictly on 18 concrete operational features captured directly at intake: service category quantities, total pairs, encoded priority, grand total, calendar intake day/month, and six binary defect indicators (scratches, yellowing, sole separation, deep stains, rips/holes, and worn-out areas)."

11. Database Design, Relational Schema & 3NF Normalization Proof

The Shoelotskey database architecture is modeled in PostgreSQL using SQLAlchemy 2.0 ORM. It strictly complies with the Third Normal Form (3NF) to eliminate data redundancy and insertion/deletion anomalies.

Normalization Form Relational Database Rule Implementation Verification in Shoelotskey SMS Status
1NF (First Normal Form) - All attributes must be atomic (no multivalued arrays or lists in columns).
- Each relation must have a primary key.
- No repeating attribute groups. 		Each table defines an unambiguous primary key (`id`, `order_id`, `user_id`). Multi-pair shoe items and multiple addons are extracted into distinct child tables (`order_items`, `order_item_addons`) rather than stored as serialized strings. ✓ 1NF Satisfied
2NF (Second Normal Form) - Must be in 1NF.
- All non-key attributes must be fully functionally dependent on the primary key (no partial dependencies). 		Single-column surrogate keys (`id`) are utilized across all primary tables. In junction tables, all non-key attributes depend entirely on the composite primary key. ✓ 2NF Satisfied
3NF (Third Normal Form) - Must be in 2NF.
- No transitive dependencies (non-key attributes depend only on the primary key, not on another non-key attribute). 		Customer details (`name`, `contact_number`, `address`) reside strictly in `customers`; `job_orders` stores only `customer_id` as a foreign key. Service pricing and categories depend strictly on `service_id` in `services`, eliminating transitive anomalies. ✓ 3NF Satisfied
